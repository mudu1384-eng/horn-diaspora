Hassan Sheikh Mohamud accuses the UAE: what was said and why it matters

Kamjaroon Online · Nov 19, 2026 · 6 min read

Somalia's president made five accusations against Abu Dhabi on 17 November, and the UAE hit back the next day. Here is what both sides said and why it matters.

On Tuesday, 17 November 2026, Somali President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud made his strongest public attack yet on the United Arab Emirates. In a recorded video address to Somalis at home and abroad, he accused Abu Dhabi of years of interference in Somalia's internal affairs. He said Somalia will defend its sovereignty, but that the door to better relations is still open.

The address comes ten months after Somalia's cabinet cancelled all agreements with the UAE on 12 January 2026.

What the president said

He made five main accusations (Dawan):

  1. Port deals without Mogadishu. The UAE signed agreements on Berbera, Bosaso and Kismayo ports directly with regional administrations, without federal approval.
  2. Military bases. The UAE set up bases and security forces outside Somalia's national security system (Türkiye Today).
  3. Backing regional leaders. Abu Dhabi gave political and financial support to some regional administrations to encourage opposition to Mogadishu.
  4. Somaliland. The UAE played a major role in Israel's recognition of Somaliland and refused to sign Arab and Muslim statements condemning it (Dawan).
  5. Berbera transit. In January the UAE secretly used Berbera to move a separatist leader fleeing Yemen, without telling Somalia's government.

He also said the UAE left Libya and Yemen divided and now wants the same for Sudan and Somalia (Dawan). He stressed that his dispute is with the Emirati government, not Emirati people or Somalis living in the UAE.

The same day, Defence Minister Ahmed Moallim Fiqi called for media outlets he said work for the UAE to be shut down. He named no outlets and gave no evidence (Dawan).

The UAE's side

The UAE answered the next day. On 18 November, Anwar Gargash, diplomatic adviser to the UAE President, said on X that the speech reflected the president's "frequent shifts" in position. He questioned whether Hassan Sheikh was putting personal interests ahead of Somalia's, and said it was regrettable that domestic power struggles were being drawn into regional disputes. He added that the UAE's ties with the Somali people run deeper than temporary political disagreements (Raxanreeb).

When Somalia accused it of interference in 2018, the UAE denied the charge. In January 2026 it signed a joint statement with the African Union supporting Somalia's sovereignty and territorial integrity (Al Jazeera).

Not all Somalis agree with Mogadishu either. After the January decision, at least three Somali regions publicly rejected cutting ties with the UAE (Africanews).

How we got here

Berbera is in Somaliland, which declared independence in 1991 but is not widely recognised. Bosaso is in Puntland and Kismayo is in Jubaland, two states that often clash with Mogadishu. Dubai-based DP World runs Berbera port under a long-term concession. The current crisis began after Israel recognised Somaliland in December 2025.

Why it matters

This touches the biggest question in Somali politics: who speaks for Somalia, the federal government or the regional states? Turkey, Egypt, Saudi Arabia and the UAE all compete for influence in the Horn.
